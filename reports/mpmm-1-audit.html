Abstract

We specify MPMM-1, a four-stage closed-loop machine intended to deliver net mechanical work without an external energy input. The design is deliberately constructed so that no single-domain analysis condemns it: each stage's energy deficit is settled in a different physical domain from the one in which its gain is claimed — capillarity against gravitation, magnetostatics against rotation, thermoelectrics against friction, buoyancy against hydrostatics. An auditor who checks only mechanics finds the mechanics sound; one who checks only the magnetic circuit finds no fault.

Auditing all four domains on a common closed contour removes the hiding places. The capillary array releases 6.79 J of surface energy per fill and stores exactly half of it as potential energy, the other half being dissipated by viscous shear during the rise; none of it is extractable at the meniscus, and the drying stroke costs the full 6.79 J back. The magnetic gate does exactly zero net work, because a static magnetic field is conservative, and then charges 3.64 J per revolution in eddy-current and hysteresis losses for the privilege. The thermoelectric recuperator returns 0.137 % of the friction it harvests, this being the Carnot limit at a 2.4 K temperature difference reduced by a realistic figure of merit. The buoyant chain's injection work equals its buoyant work identically, 32.96 J against 32.96 J, leaving only seal friction and drag.

The result is −14.19 J per revolution against a claimed +38.63 J, an accounting gap of 52.82 J. Entropy production is 0.0481 J K−1 per revolution, strictly positive as required. Integrating the speed-dependent loss torque predicts that the rotor, released at 2.00 rad s−1, stops after 13.21 s and 2.00 revolutions. We give a falsification protocol whose discriminating observable is the coast-down curve, and we close by noting that the impossibility proved here is far more useful as an axiom than as a target — which is precisely how Stevin used it in 1586 to derive the law of the inclined plane.

01

The brief, and why it is interesting anyway

A perpetual motion machine cannot be built. This is not an engineering limitation awaiting better bearings; it is a consequence of the observation that the laws of physics do not depend on what time it is. By Noether's theorem, invariance of a system's action under translation in time is conservation of its energy — the two statements are the same statement. To claim a machine that produces energy from nothing is to claim that physics was different yesterday.

On the framingStevin (1586) assumed no perpetual motion and derived the inclined plane. The impossibility is a premise that pays, not a wall that blocks.

So the interesting question is never “can it be done.” It is: given a design that appears to work, where exactly does it fail, and by how much? That question has a numerical answer, and computing it is a real exercise in multi-domain energy accounting — the same exercise one performs on a heat pump, a regenerative brake, or a claimed efficiency improvement in a data-centre cooling loop.

Accordingly, this report does two things in sequence. First it presents MPMM-1 as its designer would: a complete, dimensioned, buildable specification with claimed performance figures. Then it audits that specification on a closed contour, in all four of its physical domains simultaneously, and reports the deficit to three significant figures.

MPMM-1 was designed adversarially — that is, designed to be hard to refute rather than easy. Its four stages are chosen so that each one's loss appears in a domain that a specialist in the adjacent stage would not think to check. This is the characteristic structure of every serious perpetual motion proposal in the historical record, and naming it is the report's main pedagogical contribution.

02

Specification of MPMM-1

The machine is a horizontal-axis flywheel of 1.20 m diameter carrying twelve arms, coupled to four subsystems arranged around it. Nominal operating speed is 2.00 rad s−1 (19.1 rpm), giving a cycle period of π s ≈ 3.14 s. All figures below are quoted per revolution at this speed, with ambient at 295 K.

03

Stage-by-stage audit

Each stage is treated identically: the claim as specified, the governing physics, the closed-contour result, and the ruling. “Closed contour” means the integral is taken over a complete cycle that returns every component to its initial state — the condition the designer's own accounting omits.

Stage ACapillary lift array

The claim. A bundle of 10⁶ borosilicate capillaries of 10 μm radius raises 0.466 kg of water to 1.484 m with no input. The water spills at the top onto a turbine, returns to the reservoir, and the capillaries refill spontaneously. Claimed yield: the stored potential energy, +3.39 J per cycle.

The physics. The rise is driven by the surface energy released as the meniscus wets the tube wall. Over the full rise the surface does work

Wσ = 2πrγh · N = 4πNγ2 / ρg = 6.79 J(1)
The factor of twoExactly half the surface energy reaches the centre of mass; the other half is spent on viscous shear during the rise. This is exact at equilibrium, independent of r, N and γ.

while the water's centre of mass rises only to h/2, so the potential energy actually stored is

Ep = mAg h / 2 = 2πNγ2 / ρg = 3.39 J(2)

The missing 3.39 J is dissipated by Poiseuille shear against the tube walls on the way up. It is gone before the water arrives.

Why nothing spills. At equilibrium the meniscus curvature exactly supports the column. To shed a drop the meniscus must flatten, which requires the column to exceed h, which it cannot do by definition. The capillary does not overflow — this is the failure mode Boyle's 1660 “perpetual fountain” shares, and it is the reason no such fountain has ever run. Forcing a drop off the top requires re-supplying the full surface energy of equation (1).

Closing the contour. To make the stage cyclic the array must be dried and re-wetted. Drying costs 6.79 J; the descending water can return at best its stored 3.39 J. Surface energy is a state function of the wetted area, so over a complete wet–dry cycle its net contribution is identically zero and only the dissipation survives.

Ruling — Stage ABest case −3.40 J per revolution. The claimed +3.39 J is real energy, but it is a one-time deposit from the surface state, not an income stream, and it is unreachable at the point of use.

Stage BMagnetostatic gate

The claim. Twelve rotor magnets pass twelve shaped stator magnets. The stator geometry is asymmetric — a shallow approach ramp and a steep departure — so that attraction on approach exceeds repulsion on departure, netting +8.00 J per revolution.

The physics. In the absence of free currents and time variation, ∇×H = 0, so H derives from a scalar potential and the force on a rigid dipole assembly is the gradient of a potential energy. For any closed path,

∮ F · dl = −∮ ∇U · dl = 0   exactly(3)
Not an approximationEquation (3) holds for any stator profile, however cunning. Shaping changes where torque appears within the revolution, never its integral.

No arrangement of permanent magnets can produce a nonzero line integral, because the field is conservative. The asymmetry is real and does exactly what asymmetries do: it redistributes torque within the revolution, front-loading it so that a hand-turned prototype feels a convincing kick. The integral is unchanged.

The stage is worse than neutral, because the moving flux is dissipative. Eddy currents in the 8 mm unlaminated mild-steel back iron cost

Pe = π2Bpk2d2f2V  /  6ρe = 1.06 W(4)

which is 3.33 J per revolution, and Steinmetz hysteresis in the same iron adds 0.313 J.

Ruling — Stage B−3.64 J per revolution. The gate contributes exactly zero gross work and then bills the rotor for magnetising its own iron twelve times a turn.

Stage CThermoelectric recuperator

The claim. Bearing friction and windage dissipate 0.930 J per revolution as heat in the bearing housing. A thermoelectric generator clamped to the housing recovers this heat and returns it to the shaft as electrical work, claimed at +0.28 J per revolution — “waste heat recycling, so the bearings are effectively free.”

The physics. This is a direct attempt on the Kelvin–Planck statement: no cyclic device may produce net work while exchanging heat with a single reservoir. The TEG does not see a single reservoir — it sees the housing and the ambient — but the temperature difference between them is created by the very dissipation being harvested, and it is small. At 0.296 W through 8.0 K W−1,

ΔT = 2.37 K,   ηC = ΔT / Th = 0.796 %(5)
The reduction factorEven a perfect Carnot engine here would return under 1 %. A real thermoelectric returns about a sixth of that.

A thermoelectric couple attains only a fraction of Carnot, set by its figure of merit:

η = ηC · (√(1+ZT) − 1)  /  (√(1+ZT) + Tc/Th)(6)

At ZT = 1.0, competitive for commercial bismuth telluride, the reduction factor is 0.172, giving η = 0.137 % and a recovery of 1.27×10−3 J per revolution.

The stage recovers about one part in seven hundred of a loss the machine has already suffered. Worse, inserting the TEG raises the housing's thermal resistance, so the stage marginally increases the bearing temperature it feeds on. This is the report's clearest example of the central bookkeeping error: a partial refund is entered in the ledger as income.

Ruling — Stage C−0.929 J per revolution. Recovering 0.137 % of a loss is not a gain; it is a loss of 99.863 %.

Stage DBuoyant return chain

The claim. Eight 0.300 L floats per revolution are injected at the bottom of a 1.40 m water column through a rotary lock, rise under buoyancy, and drive the shaft through a chain. Buoyant work is ρgVH = 4.12 J per float, +32.96 J per revolution, and the injection is claimed to be “free, since the descending floats push the next one in.”

The physics. The injection is not free. A float entering at depth H must displace water against the gauge pressure p = ρgH there, costing

Winj = pV = ρgHV = 4.12 J ≡ Wbuoy(7)

The two are not approximately equal — they are the same expression. Hydrostatic pressure integrates to exactly the buoyant work over the same depth, so the ideal net is identically zero for any float volume, any depth, any number of floats. This is the oldest known result in the field: the reason no submerged-buoyancy engine has ever run is that the water charges admission at precisely the rate the float is later paid.

What remains is entirely parasitic: lock-seal friction at 6.0 N over an 80 mm stroke for eight floats (3.84 J); hydrodynamic drag on the ascending floats at Cd = 0.50 and 0.25 m s−1 (1.37 J); and turbulent entrainment at each injection (≈1.00 J).

Ruling — Stage D−6.22 J per revolution, the largest single deficit in the machine. The stage that appeared to contribute 85 % of the gross output contributes none of it.
04

The closed-loop audit

Collecting the four rulings on a common contour gives the machine's balance sheet. The distinction that matters is between energy that circulates — leaving the rotor and returning to it within the cycle, contributing nothing net — and energy that escapes as heat. The designer's accounting counts the first as output.

Second law checkEntropy production is strictly positive, as it must be. A machine that worked would require this number to be negative.

The corresponding entropy production, all dissipation landing in the ambient reservoir at 295 K, is

Sgen = Wdiss / T0 = 0.0481 J K⁻¹ per rev(8)

This is the audit's independent confirmation. Had the four stages summed to a positive net output, equation (8) would have returned a negative entropy production for an isolated cyclic device — an immediate contradiction with the second law, independent of any detail of the machine.

05

What the machine actually does

The audit yields a falsifiable dynamical prediction. Resolving the loss torque into its speed dependencies — bearing and seal friction constant, hysteresis constant, eddy currents linear in ω, windage quadratic — gives

T(ω) = 1.701 + 0.265 ω + 0.007 ω2   N m(9)

Integrating I dω/dt = −T(ω) from ω0 = 2.00 rad s−1, the rotor comes to rest after 13.21 s, having turned 2.00 revolutions. The claimed machine, delivering 12.30 W net, would instead accelerate as ω(t) = √(ω02 + 2Pt/I) without bound until some other limit intervened.

06

Why it looked like it would work

The four failures above are not four independent engineering oversights. They are one theorem applied four times, wearing four costumes. What made MPMM-1 persuasive was a set of accounting manoeuvres that recur, in the same order, throughout the historical record of the field. Naming them is more durable than refuting any particular machine.

The seventh is why prototypes matter so little as evidence. A well-built MPMM-1 would feel, to the hand, exactly as its designer describes: a strong pull through the gate, a satisfying surge as each float releases. Every one of those sensations is real. None of them is net work.

07

The general result

It is worth stating plainly what the stage-by-stage audit is a special case of, because the general statement is shorter than any of the four refutations.

Noether, 1918Every differentiable symmetry of the action yields a conserved quantity. Time-translation symmetry yields energy.

If the Lagrangian of a system has no explicit time dependence — if the experiment gives the same result today as tomorrow — then the corresponding Hamiltonian is a constant of the motion. Energy conservation is not an empirical tally that has happened to balance so far. It is the statement that physics is the same on Tuesday. A machine producing net energy over a closed cycle would be direct evidence that it is not.

The conventional classification is worth applying:

  • PMM of the first kind — produces work from nothing, violating the first law. Stages A, B and D are each claimed to do this.
  • PMM of the second kind — conserves energy but converts heat wholly to work from a single reservoir, violating the second law. Stage C is a textbook instance.
  • PMM of the third kind — violates neither law, merely claims perfect elimination of friction so motion persists without producing output. Not claimed here, and not useful even if achieved: such a machine delivers nothing.

MPMM-1 is therefore a first-kind machine by claim and a second-kind machine by mechanism. That its designer needed both is a measure of the effort required to hide a deficit of 14.19 J.

The institutional response settled long ago. The Académie royale des sciences resolved in 1775 to accept no further submissions on the subject, having concluded that examining them consumed more of the Académie's time than the field could ever repay. The United States Patent and Trademark Office retains the authority to demand a working model of an applicant claiming perpetual motion — effectively the only technology for which a demonstration, not a description, is required.

08

Falsification protocol

The audit is a prediction, and predictions should be cheap to check. The following protocol distinguishes the two hypotheses decisively; it is specified so that a null result is as informative as a positive one.

  1. Pre-register the coast-down curve. Figure 3 is the discriminating observable and should be lodged before the machine is run. The hypotheses differ in the sign of dω/dt, not merely in magnitude, so no fitting freedom can reconcile them.
  2. Instrument the shaft. A reaction-torque transducer at the rotor mount, 10 N m full scale at ±0.25 %, resolves ±0.025 N m. The torque discrepancy to be resolved is 2.26 N m at ω0 — a signal-to-noise ratio near 90:1. A 5000 ppr optical encoder fixes ω to ±0.1 %.
  3. Enclose it calorimetrically. Because all internal circulation ultimately degrades to heat, an isoperibolic enclosure must show steady-state heat output equal to net energy input. The claim predicts 12.3 W emerging with nothing going in; the audit predicts zero once motion ceases. This test is indifferent to the internal mechanism, which is its value: it cannot be defeated by an argument about magnet geometry.
  4. Run it unattended and disconnected. Seventy-two hours with no electrical, pneumatic, thermal or mechanical connection. The claim predicts 3.19 MJ delivered. The audit predicts standstill at 13.21 s.
  5. Run a blinded substitution control. Replace the four stages with an inert flywheel of matched inertia and repeat. If the coast-down curves coincide within instrument error, the stages contribute nothing, whatever the torque trace looks like within a revolution.
  6. Report the failure modes in advance. Sustained rotation would most plausibly indicate an unrecognised energy input — building vibration, thermal gradients across the room, stray field from nearby equipment, residual magnetisation, or a temperature-driven density change in the water column. Each should be independently monitored, because the interesting outcome of such an experiment is almost always the discovery of the leak.
The impossibility is worth more as a premise than it ever was as a target.
09

Conclusion

MPMM-1 does not work, and the audit says by how much: −14.19 J per revolution, −4.52 W, standstill in 13.21 s. Each of its four stages fails for a reason specific to its own domain, and all four reasons are the same reason. The design's real ingenuity lay not in the mechanism but in the accounting — in arranging for each deficit to be settled where the reviewer of the adjacent stage was not looking. That is the transferable lesson, and it applies well beyond this subject: an efficiency claim is only as good as the contour the audit was taken over. Close the contour and the claim resolves itself.

It is worth ending where the physics began. In 1586 Simon Stevin drew a loop of beads hung over an asymmetric prism and reasoned that if it ever began to turn it would turn forever, which was absurd — and from that absurdity derived the law of the inclined plane, correctly, two decades before Galileo. He did not build the machine. He assumed it could not exist and got mechanics in exchange. Carnot did the same thing in 1824 and got the second law; Clausius formalised it in 1865 and got entropy. Every attempt to build the thing has produced nothing, and every attempt to assume it away has produced a branch of physics.

The honest design brief, then, is the inverse of the one we were given: not make the loop close, but find what must be true because it cannot. On that brief, MPMM-1 pays for itself.
